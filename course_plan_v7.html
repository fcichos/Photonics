    Introduction to Photonics — Course Plan v7

    14 Lectures + 7 Seminars (14 weeks)

    Module: 12-PHY-BW3MO1 — Einführung in die Photonik I (5 LP) Target: BSc Physics, 5th semester elective (Wahlpflicht) Format: 2 SWS lecture (30 h) + 1 SWS seminar (15 h) + 105 h self-study = 150 h total Exam: Mündliche Prüfung (oral, 30 min) Prerequisite: Experimental Physics III (Exp3)

    Course Arc

    The course follows a clear narrative: from foundations → through structured media and Fourier optics → to microscopy → to scattering and sensing → to photon and quantum optics. Since Exp3 already covers basic ray optics, wave optics, and interference, Part I is a compact review that focuses on aspects beyond Exp3 (GRIN fibers, matrix methods, Lorentz model, Gaussian beams). The core of the course then builds from anisotropic media through Fourier optics to microscopy (with a consistent Fourier optics thread), and concludes with quantum optics — connecting to current research at the Molecular Nanophotonics Group.

    Part Structure

    Part I — Foundations (Lectures 1–3)

    Compact review of topics covered in Exp3, focusing on aspects that go beyond the prerequisite course.

    • L1: Introduction, Ray Optics & Wave Optics — Fermat’s principle, F=ma analogy, GRIN fibers, matrix optics, wave equation, plane/spherical waves. Review lecture — highlights connections to photonics applications.
    • L2: Interference, Coherence & EM Waves — Temporal and spatial coherence, frequency combs, light beating; EM waves in matter, Lorentz oscillator model, Clausius-Mossotti, Fresnel equations, Brewster angle, TIRF.
    • L3: Gaussian Beams — Helmholtz equation → paraxial approximation, beam propagation, ABCD matrices for beams, higher-order modes. Not covered in Exp3.

    Part II — Light in Structured Media (Lectures 4–6)

    How materials with internal structure (anisotropy, external fields, nonlinearity) manipulate light.

    • L4: Polarization & Anisotropic Media — Jones calculus, Stokes/Poincaré description; permittivity tensor, principal axes, birefringence, ordinary/extraordinary rays, wave plates. Builds on Exp3 polarization basics.
    • L5: Electro-Optics & Acousto-Optics — Pockels effect (linear EO), Kerr effect (quadratic EO), EO modulators, phase and amplitude modulation; acousto-optic Bragg diffraction, AO frequency shifting, AO modulators/deflectors. Connection to Fourier optics: AO diffraction = spatial frequency manipulation by sound waves.
    • L6: Nonlinear Optics — Anharmonic oscillator model, second-harmonic generation, phase matching, χ⁽²⁾ and χ⁽³⁾ processes, self-phase modulation, Kerr effect revisited. Natural extension of structured media: intensity-dependent response.

    Part III — Fourier Optics & Microscopy (Lectures 7–10)

    From spatial frequency analysis to advanced imaging, with a consistent Fourier optics thread.

    • L7: Fourier Optics — Fourier transform pairs, angular spectrum, free-space transfer function, spatial filtering, Abbe theory of imaging. Dense single lecture combining framework + applications.
    • L8: Microscopy I — Brightfield, Darkfield & Phase Contrast — Köhler illumination, image formation as Fourier filtering; OTF and CTF; darkfield as high-pass filter H_DF = H_BF − δ; Zernike phase contrast; DIC as differential filter.
    • L9: Microscopy II — Fluorescence & Confocal — Fluorescence fundamentals, filter cubes, widefield fluorescence; 3D OTF and missing cone; confocal pinhole, optical sectioning, confocal OTF fills missing cone; aberrations and pupil function.
    • L10: Microscopy III — Superresolution — Diffraction limit revisited; SIM (Moiré frequency mixing, 2× resolution gain); STED (PSF engineering, wider effective OTF); PALM/STORM (single-molecule localization, bypasses OTF); comparison chart.

    Part IV — Scattering & Sensing (Lectures 11–12)

    Light–nanoparticle interactions and detection methods from current research.

    • L11: Light Scattering & DLS — Rayleigh scattering, Mie theory overview, scattering cross-sections; dynamic light scattering, intensity autocorrelation, Stokes-Einstein, form factors.
    • L12: Photothermal Detection & Sensing — Absorption cross-sections, thermal lens effect, photothermal signal generation, lock-in detection, sensitivity to single nanoparticles.

    Part V — Photon & Quantum Optics (Lectures 13–14)

    The quantum nature of light — required by module description, connects to quantum technology.

    • L13: Photon Statistics — Photon streams, Poisson statistics for coherent light, beam splitters and photon statistics, thermal light and Bose-Einstein statistics, sub-Poissonian light and antibunching.
    • L14: Quantum Optics — Field quantization, quadrature components, Fock states, coherent states, beam splitter transformation, Hong-Ou-Mandel effect, entangled photons (SPDC), quantum information outlook.
