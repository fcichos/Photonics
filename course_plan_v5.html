    Introduction to Photonics — Course Plan v6

    14 Lectures + 7 Seminars (14 weeks)

    Target: 2nd/4th semester physics BSc, experimental physics elective Format: 1.5 h lecture every week + seminar every 2nd week Prerequisite: Experimental Physics III (Exp3)

    Course Arc

    The course follows a clear narrative: from waves → to imaging → to single particles → to active matter. Each block builds on the previous one, and the final lectures connect directly to current research at the Molecular Nanophotonics Group.

    Part I: Foundations (Lectures 1–5)

    Lecture 1 — Introduction & Ray Optics

    Source files: lecture01/

    • Course overview, what is photonics, where it matters
    • Ray optics & matrix (ABCD) formalism (fast recap)
    • Lenses, mirrors, optical elements
    • Demo idea: Simple ray tracing through a lens system

    Lecture 2 — Wave Optics & Interference

    Source files: lecture02/, lecture03/

    • Wave equation, superposition, complex amplitudes
    • Interference: Michelson, Fabry-Pérot, thin films
    • Coherence length, temporal and spatial coherence
    • Demo idea: White-light interferometer or soap film colours

    Lecture 3 — EM Waves & Fresnel Equations

    Source files: lecture10/

    • EM waves in matter, Maxwell’s equations recap
    • Reflection and refraction at interfaces
    • Fresnel equations, Brewster angle, total internal reflection
    • Anti-reflection coatings, dielectric mirrors

    Lecture 4 — Gaussian Beams

    Source files: lecture04/

    • Gaussian beam: waist, Rayleigh range, divergence, q-parameter
    • ABCD law for Gaussian beams (new for most students!)
    • Beam propagation through optical systems
    • MoNa connection: Focused laser beams are the workhorse of every experiment in the lab

    Lecture 5 — Polarization & Jones Calculus

    Source files: lecture07/

    • Polarization states, Jones vectors & matrices
    • Wave plates (QWP, HWP), optical activity
    • Poincaré sphere visualization
    • Applications: optical isolators, AOMs, SLMs as polarization tools
    • MoNa connection: Polarization control is essential for microscopy and optical trapping setups

    Part II: Fourier Optics (Lectures 6–7)

    Lecture 6 — Fourier Optics I

    Source files: lecture05/

    • Fourier transform review, spatial frequencies
    • Angular spectrum propagation
    • Fresnel & Fraunhofer diffraction
    • Lens as Fourier transformer

    Lecture 7 — Fourier Optics II: Spatial Filtering & Resolution

    Source files: lecture06/

    • PSF, OTF/MTF, Abbe diffraction limit
    • Spatial filtering: low-pass, high-pass, band-pass
    • SLM-based wavefront shaping (practical application of Fourier optics)
    • Demo idea: Optical Fourier filtering with laser and lenses

    Part III: Microscopy as Applied Fourier Optics (Lectures 8–11)

    The microscopy block treats all imaging modalities through the lens of Fourier optics: image formation as double Fourier transform, contrast techniques as pupil-plane manipulations, resolution as OTF cutoff, and superresolution as extending the passband.

    Lecture 8 — Microscopy I: Brightfield & Darkfield

    Source files: lecture09/01

    • Abbe’s imaging theory — image formation as double Fourier transform
    • OTF and CTF: incoherent OTF (autocorrelation of pupil, cutoff \(2\text{NA}/\lambda\)) vs. coherent CTF (pupil itself, cutoff \(\text{NA}/\lambda\))
    • Köhler illumination, coherent vs. incoherent imaging
    • Brightfield phase problem: phase info lost in intensity detection
    • Darkfield as high-pass filter: \(H_\text{DF} = H_\text{BF} - \delta\)
    • MoNa connection: These are the microscopes we use every day

    Lecture 9 — Microscopy II: Phase Contrast & Fluorescence

    Source files: lecture09/02

    • Phase contrast (Zernike): phase shift of DC in pupil plane → converts phase to amplitude
    • DIC as differential filter in Fourier space: \(H_\text{DIC}(k) \propto k\)
    • Fluorescence: Jablonski diagram, Stokes shift, filter cubes
    • 3D OTF and the missing cone: widefield fluorescence has poor axial frequency transfer
    • Confocal: product of illumination × detection OTFs → fills the missing cone
    • Two-photon: nonlinear \(I^2\) excitation → equivalent confocal OTF
    • MoNa connection: Fluorescence is how we see single molecules

    Lecture 10 — Confocal Microscopy & Wavefront Control

    Source files: lecture09/03

    • Confocal OTF revisited: convolution of illumination and detection OTFs
    • Pinhole as spatial frequency filter; AOMs for fast beam scanning
    • Aberrations as pupil-plane phase errors: \(P = A \exp(iW)\) degrades OTF
    • Strehl ratio and Maréchal approximation
    • Zernike polynomials: orthonormal basis on unit disk for wavefront decomposition
    • Wavefront sensing (Shack-Hartmann): measures \(\nabla W\), i.e., \(ik\) in Fourier space — same math as DIC
    • Adaptive optics: SLM-based correction, deformable mirrors, sensorless AO
    • MoNa connection: SLMs for beam shaping, holographic trapping, and aberration correction

    Lecture 11 — Superresolution Microscopy

    Source files: lecture12/

    • The diffraction barrier as OTF cutoff at \(2\text{NA}/\lambda\)
    • SIM: frequency mixing via Moiré effect extends passband by factor 2
    • STED: nonlinear PSF engineering → wider effective OTF
    • PALM/STORM: bypasses OTF via single-molecule localization (\(\sigma \propto 1/\sqrt{N}\))
    • MINFLUX: structured illumination + single-molecule detection for nm precision
    • MoNa connection: Single-molecule imaging is central to our research

    Part IV: Modern Photonics (Lectures 12–14)

    Lecture 12 — Light Scattering & Dynamic Light Scattering

    Source files: lecture14/

    • Why is the sky blue? Rayleigh scattering (∝ λ⁻⁴)
    • Mie scattering: when particles are comparable to λ
    • Dynamic light scattering (DLS): measuring particle sizes from fluctuations
    • Autocorrelation function, Stokes-Einstein equation

    Lecture 13 — Photothermal Detection & Sensing

    Source files: lecture13/

    • Photothermal microscopy (core MoNa research!):
      • Absorbing nanoparticle → local heating → refractive index change → phase shift
      • Pump-probe scheme, lock-in detection with AOMs
      • Single gold nanoparticle detection (label-free!)
      • Sensitivity: from 40 nm particles down to single molecules
    • MoNa connection: This is our flagship technique — show real photothermal images and signals

    Lecture 14 — From Photons to Motion: Microswimmers & Active Matter

    Source files: lecture16/

    • Optical forces: radiation pressure, gradient force, optical tweezers
    • Thermophoresis: particles moving along temperature gradients
    • Self-thermophoresis: Janus particles with asymmetric heating
    • Photon nudging: using real-time feedback to steer microswimmers
    • Hot Brownian motion: what happens when the particle is hotter than the bath?
    • Active matter: collective behaviour, machine learning for micro-machines
    • MoNa connection: This IS our current research frontier

    Seminar Details

    Each seminar combines pen-and-paper calculations with Python exercises.

    Seminar 1 — Wave Optics Calculations (Week 2, after L2)

    • Ray tracing through lens systems (ABCD matrices)
    • Interference fringe spacing calculations (Michelson, thin films)
    • Fresnel reflection at glass-air interface
    • Python: Simulate multi-beam interference patterns

    Seminar 2 — Gaussian Beams & Jones Calculus (Week 4, after L4)

    • Gaussian beam propagation through a lens system
    • Finding beam waist after focusing objectives
    • Jones matrices: linear polarizer, QWP, HWP combinations
    • Python: Polarization state visualization on Poincaré sphere

    Seminar 3 — Fourier Optics Workshop (Week 6, after L6)

    • Fourier transform of simple apertures (slit, circular, grating)
    • Fraunhofer diffraction pattern calculations
    • Design a spatial filter to remove specific frequencies
    • Python: 2D FFT of microscopy images, low-pass filtering

    Seminar 4 — Microscopy Lab Calculations (Week 8, after L8)

    • Calculate resolution limits for given NA and λ
    • PSF width for different objective types
    • Compare contrast: brightfield vs. darkfield vs. phase contrast
    • Fluorescence: estimate signal levels for given quantum yield
    • Python: Simulate Abbe imaging and Fourier filtering

    Seminar 5 — Superresolution Techniques (Week 10, after L10)

    • STED: calculate effective PSF for given depletion intensity
    • Localization precision: Thompson formula, photon budget
    • SIM: frequency space construction (3 orientations, 5 raw images)
    • Python: Monte Carlo PALM/STORM simulation

    Seminar 6 — Scattering & Photothermal Experiments (Week 12, after L12)

    • Rayleigh scattering cross-section for gold nanoparticles
    • DLS: extract diffusion coefficient from autocorrelation, Stokes-Einstein
    • Photothermal signal estimation: absorption → temperature → phase shift
    • SNR analysis: when can you see a single nanoparticle?
    • Python: Fit DLS correlation functions, simulate photothermal signals

    Seminar 7 — Active Matter Workshop & Course Review (Week 14, after L14)

    • Langevin equation for a Brownian particle
    • Mean-square displacement: passive vs. active (ballistic regime)
    • Thermophoretic drift velocity estimation
    • Photon nudging: optimal switching strategy
    • Python: Simulate and analyze microswimmer trajectories
    • Course wrap-up: Review key concepts, Q&A for exam preparation
